# Approved findings

Status: candidate-approved

These findings are based on the prepared 2021-2025 workforce outputs and the currently downloaded external-data vintage. They are descriptive. They do not claim that economic conditions caused workforce outcomes.

## 1. New-hire retention generally met its objective

Across all measurable six-month cohorts, 1,576 of 1,808 new hires were retained through their six-month anniversary. The resulting 87.2% retention rate is above the 86% objective.

The objective was not met consistently in every view:

- 2023 retention was 85.9%, slightly below the objective.
- Sales retention across the full measured period was 85.7%, also slightly below the objective.
- Supply Chain, Digital, and Finance were above the objective across the full measured period.

The 2025 rate is 90.7%, but it includes only cohorts whose six-month anniversaries had occurred by the workforce data's as-of date. It is therefore a partial-year cohort result, not a final full-year outcome.

## 2. Senior-hire retention is the clearest workforce concern

Across all measurable twelve-month senior cohorts, 209 of 266 senior hires were retained through their twelve-month anniversary. The resulting 78.6% retention rate is below the 90% objective.

Every country and every business unit was below 90% when aggregated across the full measurable period. This makes senior-hire retention the clearest objective-level concern in the supplied data.

The result requires caution because senior cohorts are small. All 90 country-quarter observations used in the quarterly economic comparisons carry `SMALL_SAMPLE`. A single departure can therefore move an individual quarterly percentage substantially. The overall multi-period pattern is more defensible than any single senior country-quarter result.

There is no measurable 2025 twelve-month senior-cohort result because those hires had not yet completed their twelve-month anniversaries. They are not treated as failures.

## 3. Regretted turnover generally remained within target

The annual organisation-level results remained below the maximum 7.5% objective:

| Year | Regretted turnover |
| --- | ---: |
| 2021 | 4.3% |
| 2022 | 3.7% |
| 2023 | 3.3% |
| 2024 | 3.2% |
| 2025 | 5.0% |

Turnover increased in 2025 after declining through 2024. It still met the objective, so this is a monitoring signal rather than an objective failure.

At country-quarter level, 108 of 120 observations met the target and 12 exceeded it. Healthy annual totals can therefore coexist with particular country-quarter exceptions that deserve investigation.

## 4. The selected economic indicators do not provide a clear overall explanation

Most of the nine Spearman scores are close to zero, so the supplied data does not show a consistent ordering between workforce outcomes and unemployment, vacancies, or inflation.

The score furthest from zero is regretted turnover versus trailing job vacancies at -0.263. In this dataset, periods with higher trailing vacancy rates tended to have somewhat lower turnover rates. This is an association only and does not establish that job vacancies reduced turnover.

Inflation has almost no monotonic association with annual senior-hire retention (-0.003) or annual regretted turnover (-0.002). This lack of a clear relationship is a valid non-finding and prevents an unsupported economic explanation.

## Interpretation boundaries

- Correlation does not establish causation.
- Countries appear repeatedly across time and are not independent experiments.
- Senior-hire cohorts are small and their individual quarterly rates are unstable.
- Early 2021 trailing economic windows include the exceptional 2020 COVID-19 period.
- National indicators may not describe the local conditions experienced by each employee.
- Current external values may include revisions published after the workforce period.
- Business unit is assumed to remain constant because no transfer history was supplied.
- A target met in an aggregate does not mean every country, quarter, or business unit met it.
